# Pizza Stone Dough — 3 × 13-inch Pizzas

**Mixer:** KitchenAid Classic K45SS with dough hook  
**Yield:** 3 × 13-inch pizzas  
**Rise:** About 90 minutes  
**Yeast:** Active dry yeast

## Equipment

- KitchenAid Classic K45SS mixer
- Dough hook
- Large bowl for rising
- Bowl cover, plastic wrap, or clean kitchen towel
- Pizza stone
- Pizza peel or rimless baking sheet for transferring pizzas
- Parchment paper or flour/cornmeal for the peel, as preferred
- Measuring cups and measuring spoons
- Oven capable of reaching about 500°F

## Ingredients

- 4 cups bread flour (about 500–520 g)
- 1 1/3 cups warm water (105–110°F)
- 2 1/4 tsp active dry yeast (1 packet)
- 1 tsp sugar
- 1 tsp salt
- 3 tbsp olive oil

## Steps

1. **Bloom the yeast:** Add the warm water, sugar, and active dry yeast to the KitchenAid bowl. Stir briefly and let sit for **5–10 minutes**, until foamy.
2. Add the **3 tbsp olive oil**.
3. Add the **4 cups flour** and **1 tsp salt**.
4. Attach the dough hook. Mix on **Speed 2 for about 2 minutes**, until the dough comes together.
5. Continue kneading on **Speed 2 for 6–8 minutes**. The finished dough should be smooth, elastic, and slightly tacky.
6. Form into a ball and place in a lightly oiled bowl. Cover and let rise for **about 90 minutes**, until noticeably puffy/doubled.
7. Divide the dough evenly into **3 dough balls**.
8. Cover and let the dough balls rest for **15–20 minutes** so they relax and stretch easily.
9. Preheat the pizza stone thoroughly at your oven's highest practical pizza temperature (typically **500°F**) for at least **45 minutes**.
10. Stretch each dough ball into a **13-inch pizza**. Add sauce, cheese, and toppings.
11. Bake directly on the hot pizza stone until the crust is browned and the cheese is bubbling, typically **7–10 minutes at 500°F**, depending on toppings and your oven.

## Notes

- This is the quick, same-day pizza dough: the **2 1/4 tsp yeast** is intentional for the roughly **90-minute rise**.
- Bread flour gives the best chew and structure, but all-purpose flour can be substituted.
- If the dough keeps shrinking while stretching, leave it alone for another **5–10 minutes** and try again.
